Help now, adapt later

Can an AI assistant make a community better at today's problem while leaving it worse at the next one, and what keeps an LLM assistant that keeps learning from the community it advises from making everyone the same? We test both in the NK model of Fazelpour, O'Brien & Rubin (2026).

Where it stands

RQ1 · Replication

Our build reproduces the paper.

NP cuts diversity by 23.8% (paper: 25%), helps only at high modularity and low use; TDR beats SBP at every use rate when ρ = 1; SBP helps everywhere. NP's gains start at ρ ≈ 0.9 rather than 0.8.

RQ2 · Help now vs later

How an assistant looks while in use is a poor guide to what it leaves behind.

At ρ = 1, use 0.5, after 30 rounds: PCC (a tailored full answer) is +43.6% while assisted and +5.3% after a shift with the AI gone; SBP (a one-step nudge) is +18.4% and +4.2%; NP is +23% and −2%. Rankings flip in 6–16 settings per condition at Bonferroni p < 6×10⁻⁵.

RQ3 · Mechanism and robustness

Whole copied solutions homogenise and pay at the shift; nudges do not. This holds with a denser network, a 50% shift and K = 5.

Shift penalty vs diversity lost: r = 0.63, 0.52, 0.48, 0.49 across the four conditions. With a 50% shift, PCC drops from +43.6% to −1.3% and SBP is the only assistant still ahead of no AI.

RQ4 · Does a longer reward fix it?

No. Being able to step back does.

A policy choosing full answer, nudge or step back learns the same thing under R_now, R_long and R_adapt (two ES seeds each): help hard for ~5 rounds, then step back ~85% of the time. At ρ = 1: +86% while assisted, +26 to +28% after the shift, versus +68% / +24% for the best rule that always answers. A one-line rule (help only if it improves the asker, with whichever kind gains more, else step back) gets +85% / +25%.

RQ5 · Over continual problems

Once the community is uniform, assistants have little left to give. The gated rule holds up best.

Four problems in a row, fitness while assisted vs no AI. PCC: +27.8%, then +4.0%, −3.6%, +2.7% (the last three not significant). SBP: +13.2%, +5.6%, +3.4%, +4.1%. Gated: +58.7%, +17.4%, +6.1%, +6.0%, the only one clearly ahead in every problem. Copying from peers offers nothing once every peer holds the same solution.

RQ6 · LLM learning online

The model collapses onto one answer for nearly everyone within the first problem. A KL anchor to the base model is the only lever tested that keeps its answers varied.

36 runs, 6 seeds per arm at ρ = 1 and 0.8. Late in each problem, 83–100% of askers get the same answer from R_now and from step back, with 1.0–1.6 distinct answers in a group of 8. Step back collapses onto always stepping back or onto one answer. KL keeps 2.2–4.1 distinct and 49–77% on the top answer (KL − R_now: +1.9 distinct, p = .007 at ρ = 0.8; +1.5, p = .07 at ρ = 1). The effect on the community is not resolved: the LLM is a weak assistant here (about 40% of the oracle nudge's early gain at ρ = 1, 10% at ρ = 0.8, within a few percent of no AI over a problem), and 6 seeds give CIs of ±20–80 points after the first problem.

How an LLM could break the curse

An assistant that keeps learning from the community it advises gets hit twice. Its answers make people alike (P1b). Then it learns from people it has already made alike, so its own answers narrow, and after a shift it has nothing varied left to learn from. The oracle runs show the first half: help that recombines what people already have runs dry. These are the levers, with what the simulations say so far:

LeverWhat the sims sayIn P3
Reward that sees the community later (R_adapt)No effect once stepping back is allowed (P2)On hold

P3 measures the curse directly. Across askers: does the model give everyone the same answer? Within a group of 8 samples: does it stop varying, which leaves GRPO no signal? In the community: does diversity collapse? After each shift: how fast do model and community recover?

Framing

Where the story stands

Sims: help that looks good during use can leave people worse at the next problem; the cause is whole copied answers; a longer reward does not fix it, stepping back does. LLM: an assistant trained online on its users collapses onto one answer, and only a KL anchor keeps it varied. The missing piece is whether that collapse costs the community, which G-3 (a) answers.
