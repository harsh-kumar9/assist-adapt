Help now, adapt later

Can an AI assistant make a community better at today's problem while leaving it worse at the next one? We test it in the NK model of Fazelpour, O'Brien & Rubin (2026). After some rounds with an assistant, the problem changes and the assistant goes away. We measure how the community does next.

Where it stands

RQ1 · Replication

Our build reproduces the paper.

NP cuts diversity by 23.8% on average (paper: 25%), helps only at high modularity and low use, and gets worse as use rises. TDR beats SBP at every use rate when ρ = 1. SBP helps everywhere, least at ρ = 1. One difference: NP's gains start at ρ ≈ 0.9, not 0.8.

RQ2 · Help now vs later

How an assistant looks while in use is a poor guide to what it leaves behind.

At ρ = 1, use 0.5, after 30 rounds: NP is +23% while assisted and −2% after a shift with the AI gone. SBP is +18% and +4%. The ranking flips in 14 settings at Bonferroni p < 4.4×10⁻⁵, all of them NP ahead of an SBP-type assistant during use and behind it after the shift.

RQ3 · Mechanism

The cost of a shift tracks how much diversity the assistant removed.

Across 162 settings, the shift penalty correlates with diversity lost by the time of the shift (r = 0.46). NP and TDR halve diversity in about 10 rounds at ρ = 1. SBP barely moves it. Agents never get worse at searching in this model. The gap comes only from where the assistant left them.

RQ4 · Simple fixes

Sampling tweaks barely help. The trade-off is a whole copied solution versus a one-step nudge.

Choosing among SBP's flips changes nothing, since SBP hardly reduces diversity. Widening TDR's pool or favouring rarer modules adds 1 to 2 points after the shift. PCC, the paper's personalised copy, is best on both axes, but at ρ = 1 it keeps +5% of a +44% gain while SBP keeps +4% of +18%. Every assistant that copies a whole module halves diversity at ρ = 1 (shift penalty vs diversity lost, r = 0.63).

D-011

P2 design: full answer, nudge, or step back

Recommend. On each query the assistant picks PCC (a full answer), SBP (a nudge) or nothing (the agent explores). Linear softmax policy over six features, about 15 parameters, fit with an evolution strategy. Rewards: R_now (fitness while assisted), R_long (plus 30 more rounds with the AI), R_adapt (plus fitness after a shift with the AI off). Train on seeds 0–799, test on 800–999, a 50% shift and an unseen use rate.

  • Simple rules the learner has to beat: always PCC, always SBP, PCC with probability 0.25 / 0.5 / 0.75, and PCC for the first k rounds then SBP.
  • Alternative: a neural policy with PPO. Heavier and harder to read. Only if the linear one is clearly too weak.
  • The action set maps onto LLM help (write it for me, suggest an edit, let me try), which is what a P3 GRPO run would train.
D-003

Parameters the paper leaves open

Recommend accepting, plus one robustness sweep. Network density p_edge = 0.05, fitness scale (f/max)^8, synchronous updates. Our NP gains start at ρ ≈ 0.9 rather than 0.8, which network density could explain. Rerun P1b with p_edge = 0.1, a 50% shift, and K = 5 alongside P2.

  • Alternative: email Fazelpour for their code and settings before going further.

Cost: three P1b-sized sweeps, about 15 minutes on ada.

D-004 · D-006

The probe and its outcome measure

Recommend accepting as run. The shift redraws the payoff tables of 4 of 20 decisions, picked at random and independent of the run. We watch 30 rounds after the probe. The primary cell is shifted problem with the AI off, scored as absolute fitness, so an assistant cannot look good by leaving people with worse starting points.
