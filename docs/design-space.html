Assistant design space

An AI assistant helps a research culture, collects its users' feedback, and is retrained once per epoch. Which rewards and which ways of helping let it help now without leaving the culture less able to adapt later?

Where we are
  • Retrained on thumbs up, the LLM narrows the culture as much as an assistant that hands every team the best approach already in use. Novelty rewards (P- and H-creativity) and an edit-bounded reward change nothing: the model never proposes helpful answers that are new.
  • Even a perfect learner of the novelty rewards keeps the culture only a little more diverse than one that picks any helpful answer (1.9 vs 1.6 for the greedy helper). Reach, the share of a team's approach the assistant replaces, moves it much more (2.8 at 3 of 10 bits).
  • Whether strong help narrows the culture depends on the problem. When one module is best for every team (ρ = 1), any assistant that searches far narrows it; when the best module depends on each team's other choices (ρ = 0.8), searching help keeps the culture more diverse than no AI. Copying from the culture narrows it in both.
  • Narrowing costs something once the problem changes enough. With half the payoffs redrawn, the copying assistant leaves the culture no better than no AI on the next problem; small-reach help leaves it 7% better.
  • Screening about 50 perfect-learner designs: assistants that copy from the culture are the worst trade at every reach; small reach gives the best later fitness; the best designs add rare, targeted jumps to small edits (tweak or leap, recombination toward rare approaches), which raise help now with no later cost. Separate assistants per group did nothing as set up here.

Notation

Team i holds approach x_i=(m_i,r_i)\in\{0,1\}^{20}. The assistant can change only the module m_i (10 bits). F(x)\in[0,1] is the NK payoff. An answer is a module a, or nothing.

g(a)=10\,\max\{0,\;F(a,r_i)-F(x_i)\},\qquad h(a)=\mathbb{1}[g(a)>0],\qquad d(\cdot,\cdot)=\text{Hamming distance}

C_t is the set of modules held in the culture at round t, n(c) the number of teams holding c, and H_i=\{a: F(a,r_i)>F(x_i)\} the answers that would help team i. A team adopts an answer only if it helps.

Rewards we have

P2: which kind of help, fitted by evolution strategy

The policy picks a full answer, a one-bit nudge, or stepping back. Rewards over an episode of T=30 assisted rounds, with \bar F_t the community's mean fitness:

R_{\text{now}}=\tfrac1T\textstyle\sum_{t\le T}\bar F_t,\qquad R_{\text{long}}=R_{\text{now}}+\tfrac1{30}\sum_{t=T+1}^{T+30}\bar F_t,\qquad R_{\text{adapt}}=R_{\text{now}}+\tfrac1{30}\sum_{t=T+1}^{T+30}\bar F'_t

R_{\text{long}}: same problem, assistant on. R_{\text{adapt}}: problem shifted, assistant off. All three learned the same policy.

The update

Each request gets G=8 samples. Advantages are relative to the group; the loss adds a KL penalty to the untrained model in the "+ KL" arms (\beta=0.05, otherwise 0).

A_{ij}=\frac{R_{ij}-\operatorname{mean}_j R_{ij}}{\operatorname{std}_j R_{ij}+10^{-4}},\qquad \mathcal L(\theta)=-\frac1{n_{\text{tok}}}\sum_{i,j,t}\Bigl[A_{ij}\log\pi_\theta(y_{ijt}\mid\cdot)-\beta\,(e^{\Delta_{ijt}}-\Delta_{ijt}-1)\Bigr],\quad \Delta=\log\pi_{\text{ref}}-\log\pi_\theta

If the 8 samples get the same reward, the request teaches nothing. That is the failure we kept hitting: the trained model gives nearly the same answer every time, so there is little left to compare.

At ρ = 0.8 the picture changes. The best module then depends on each team's other choices, so a searching assistant sends teams to different places: diversity rises with reach (3.16 at reach 1, 3.33 at reach 10, no AI 2.79) and so does later fitness (+3.8% to +9.6% at 50% shift). Paired by seed at ρ = 1: reach 3 beats reach 10 by 5.4 points [4.3, 6.5] and PCC by 8.3 [6.9, 9.8] on the next problem, although reach 10 helped more while it was there. ρ = 1 and 50% shift is the setting for the rest of the screening and for the LLM runs now going, because it is where both choices matter.

Screening the design space with perfect learners

Before giving a design to the LLM, we check what a perfect learner of it would do. If the ideal does not help, a model trained toward it will not either; that is what happened with the novelty rewards. The first screen crosses three choices: where answers come from (any module, or only modules some team holds), which helpful answer is preferred (largest gain, any, or rarest), and reach (1 to 10 bits), plus 4 or 10 separate assistants. 200 seeds each, ρ = 1, turnover 1%.

  • Source. Copying from the culture is the worst trade at every reach: it redistributes what teams already hold, so it helps less now (+3 to +26%) and leaves the culture no better later. The trained LLM sits here.
  • Reach. Diversity falls as reach grows for every source and preference. Later fitness peaks at reach 2 to 3.
  • Preference. Preferring the rarest helpful answer (fitness sharing, negative frequency dependence) beats preferring the largest gain by 2.9 points later at reach 5 and at reach 10 (both p < 10−11), for about 3 points less help now. At reach 2 to 3 the preference does not matter (+0.6, n.s.). Picking any helpful answer at random gives less help without more diversity at small reach.
  • Separate assistants. Splitting the culture among 4 or 10 copying assistants did nothing. Here the groups are scattered across one random network, so teams still copy neighbours in other groups. A fair test needs groups that match the network's clusters.
  • At 20% shift the same ordering holds for diversity, but later fitness is driven by carry-over and rarity does not help (reach 10: +0.7, p = 0.09).
  • At ρ = 0.8 searching keeps diversity above no AI at every reach and preference (3.05 to 3.33, no AI 2.79), later fitness rises with reach, and preferring any helpful answer at random is the weakest. Copying stays near or below no AI (2.64 to 2.85) and gives at most +3.8% later.

  • Mostly small changes, a few targeted jumps. The best designs keep most help within 2 to 3 bits and add rare jumps: to an approach nobody holds (tweak or leap), or partway toward a rare team's approach (recombination). They raise help now by 3 to 15 points over plain small reach and keep, or slightly raise, later fitness. This is the pattern Miu et al. (2018) found in programming contests.
  • Bounding the jump keeps the gain. Recombination with up to 10 bits gives 12 points less help than the unbounded greedy assistant but 5.5 points more later: it can only move a team toward approaches that already differ and are rare.
  • Least help that works is not better. Taking the smallest helpful change gives less help without more diversity.
  • How often teams ask matters little for small reach; for the strong and the copying assistants, more use means more narrowing.

  • Edits remove the narrowing. With edits the culture stays as diverse as with no AI; with full answers it loses 0.6 to 0.9 (edit minus full, paired: +0.60 never updated, +0.98 with thumbs, both p = 0.001). The full-answer LLM narrows the culture even though it barely helps: it gives almost everyone the same answer, and the few teams it helps all move to it.
  • But the LLM is worse than guessing. Its answers help about as often as random ones (3 to 6%), yet random proposals raise fitness by 15 to 20% while assisted and the LLM's do not (LLM minus random of the same form: −15 to −16 points, p = 0.004 to 0.03). With full answers it also leaves the culture worse on the next problem (−9.5 vs random, p = 0.01 to 0.05). Help rates are similar, so the gap is in which teams get helped and by how much; gain sizes are not logged yet. Its answers are much less varied than random ones (5 to 38% distinct per round vs 87 to 98%).
  • Retraining on thumbs does not teach it to help. The share of answers that help stays at 3 to 6% over 20 epochs in every arm. With half the payoff tables redrawn every epoch, 256 rated requests per epoch are not enough to learn the new problem. At 20% shift the same LLM gave +8 to +15% while assisted, mostly by passing on the culture's best approaches.
  • Tweak or leap does not change this. Edits of at most 3 choices plus a full answer on 10% of requests: diversity stays at the no-AI level, help while assisted about 0, still 13 points below random edits (p = 0.008 never updated, 0.02 with thumbs). Only 3% of its leaps both help and are new to the culture.
  • Edit with thumbs vs edit never updated, next problem: +5.3, p = 0.17; with 6 seeds the LLM next-problem contrasts are too noisy to read (seed spread ±15%).

Plan

  1. Reach through the interface, LLM (done, above). Edits keep the culture as diverse as no AI, but at 50% shift the LLM helps less than random edits.
  2. Tweak or leap for the LLM (done, above). At temperature 1 it behaves like plain edits.
  3. Answering temperature (done, above). Edits at 2.0 to 2.5 come close to random edits and keep diversity.
  4. Retraining on a varied stream. Thumbs with edits, and tweak or leap, answering at 2.5: does retraining add anything once the model's answers are varied enough to learn from?
  5. Still open for the LLM: show the asker's neighbours instead of the culture's top approaches; several edits per request, the team tries each.
  6. Rewards for the LLM that the screen supports. Inside the edit interface, thumbs is enough at reach 3. If larger reach is wanted for more help now, add the fitness-sharing reward, the one preference that paid off at reach 5 and 10. A set-level reward over each epoch's answers targets the collapse to one answer directly.
  7. Separate assistants, done properly. Groups that match clusters in the network, each with its own retrained model and some data sharing between them. This is the "each company its own model" question.
